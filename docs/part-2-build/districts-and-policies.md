# Districts and policies

> *Paint a district to apply its local development policy.*

A district is a painted area of the map with a name and a local policy.
Districts are how you give a neighborhood its own character — a historic
low-rise core, a high-density downtown, a green residential area.

## The district brush

Open the **Districts** tool from the build menu. You'll see a brush that
paints a coloured overlay on the map, and a small panel where you name
the district and pick its policy.

> *Paint a district to apply its local development policy.*

### How to paint
1. Open the **Districts** tool.
2. Pick a brush size.
3. Click or drag to paint the area.
4. Give the district a name.
5. Pick a policy.
6. Save.

> *Brush size* sets the area of each click. The whole city can be one
> district, or you can have a dozen small ones.

### The default policy: open
- *OPEN LAND* — no special rules. Buildings grow as the city asks.

## Local policies

Lundby has a small library of local policies, each with a clear effect on
the painted area.

### Historic district · low-rise only
*Historic district · low-rise only*

- Restricts new buildings to low rise.
- Useful for preserving a heritage core.
- *Place 100 improvements* — a milestone for restoring heritage
  buildings.

### High-rise district
*High-rise district*

- Encourages tall buildings.
- Office and residential towers prefer this district.
- *A neighborhood in the sky* — the flavor name for a dense cluster.

### Business district
*Business district*

- Favors commercial and office uses.
- *Zone connected land with utilities to start a new business district.*

### Central district
*Central district*

- A mix of uses with priority for civic and commercial buildings.
- *Capital City* — the flavor name for the densest central district.

### Industrial
- Favors industrial uses.
- *Move heavy industry away from homes and add parks or clean industry
  standards.*

### Old town
*Old town*

- A low-rise mix of residential and small commercial.
- *A REASON TO STAY* — the flavor name for an old town district.

### Waterfront
*Waterfront*

- Favors waterfront destinations.
- *A waterfront destination. Must be close to the shore.*

### High water
*High water*

- Flags land that's at risk of flooding.
- Useful for planning evacuation and flood defences.

### Open land
- The default policy. No special rules.

## Districting tips

- **Districts don't override zoning.** A high-rise district still
  respects the zone you paint inside it — it just makes the buildings
  taller.
- **Districts can overlap.** A waterfront residential district on top of
  a historic district means "low-rise waterfront."
- **Districts are easy to repaint.** If your downtown should be denser,
  paint over it with the high-rise policy.
- **Names matter for organization.** "Downtown" and "Old town" are
  easier to find in reports than "District 1."

## Tips & common pitfalls

- **Don't paint a tiny district.** The simulation works at the
  neighborhood level; a one-block district has little effect.
- **Do name your districts.** Reports and overlays use the name; an
  unnamed district is harder to track.
- **Districts are policy, not zoning.** They guide the city; they don't
  enforce it.
- **A historic district is not a museum.** Existing buildings stay
  even if they're not low-rise; only new construction respects the cap.
