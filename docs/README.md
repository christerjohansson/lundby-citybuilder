# The Mayor's Handbook

> *A city, shaped by you.*

Welcome home, Mayor.

Lundby is a 3D city-building simulation that runs in your browser. You zone
neighborhoods, lay roads and rail, keep the lights on and the books balanced,
and grow a coastal village into a living region — at your own pace, in your
own time, on the terrain of your choice.

This handbook is your complete reference. It is organised the way you play:

- **Part I** gets you from the title screen to your first block of zoning.
- **Parts II–IV** cover what you build, what keeps it running, and what it costs.
- **Part V** explains the simulation that hums beneath it all.
- **Part VI** is the interface — every panel, every report, every map.
- **Part VII** covers saving, loading, photo mode, and offline play.
- **Appendix A** is for the curious — a peek at the project itself.

## How to read this book

Each page follows the same shape:

1. **What it is** — the concept in two or three sentences.
2. **How to use it** — the steps you'll actually take in-game.
3. **Key controls** — buttons, shortcuts, and overlays.
4. **Tips & pitfalls** — the small things that make a big difference.
5. **Related pages** — where to go next.

Where a screenshot would help, you'll find an image placeholder. The alt text
describes exactly what the shot should show so the screenshot pass is
mechanical.

## A few things to keep in mind

- **Lundby is a city, not a clock.** Sim time moves at its own pace, day by
  day. You can pause, advance a single day, or change simulation speed.
- **Demand drives growth.** Zone where the demand bars say to, and your city
  will fill in on its own.
- **Connected is everything.** Roads, power, water, transit, and regional
  links all need a continuous path. A single broken tile can stall a service.
- **The region is shared.** Once you connect to a neighbouring municipality,
  you can share services, trade, and even run shared regional projects.

## A note on voice

Lundby talks to you. You'll see flavor lines like *"A destination, an
economy, a home."* and *"Welcome home, Mayor."* This book keeps that voice —
because the city is part of the game.
