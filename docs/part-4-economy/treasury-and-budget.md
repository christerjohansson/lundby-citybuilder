# Treasury and budget

> *Every dollar counts.*

The treasury is the city's bank account. Every line item in the game
flows through it: taxes, fees, maintenance, payroll, grants, and bonds.
The budget panel is your window into the city's finances.

## The treasury

The **Treasury** is the running balance of your city's cash. It's
settled once a month.

> *Treasury* / *Cash & bills* / *CITY FUNDS*

### What flows in
- **Residential & business taxes** — collected monthly.
- *Tax receipts* / *Taxes received* / *Tax receipts earned this
> month.*
- *Assessed housing tax* — the residential line.
- *Business tax assessment* — the commercial line.
- *Average household tax bill* — the per-household metric.
- *Earned tourism receipts* — visitor spending.
- *Earned airport passenger fees* — the airport's contribution.
- *Casino concession receipts* / *Hotel concession receipts* — the
> attraction lines.
- *Export receipts* / *Export received* / *EXPORT RECEIPTS · THIS
> MONTH* — exports.
- *Completed export receipts* — the closed-out line.
- *Commuter fares* / *Public transport fares, received* — transit
> revenue.
- *Fares received this month* / *Passenger fares collected* — the
> transit lines.
- *Visitor receipts* / *Visitor arrival fee* — tourism.
- *Milestone grant* / *Financial aid received* — the windfall lines.
- *Bond paid off* — when a bond matures.
- *Returned-load refunds* / *Returned factory cargo refunds* / *Returned
> catalyst cargo refunds* — the small refunds that add up.
- *Regional freight receipts* / *Regional supplies received* — the
> shared lines.

### What flows out
- **Road & rail maintenance** — every tile, every month.
- **Service funding** — fire, police, hospitals, schools, transit.
- *Service per staffed position* — the per-unit cost.
- *Maintenance / month* — the per-building cost.
- *Monthly maintenance* — the all-buildings total.
- *Pay per worker / month* — the workforce cost.
- *Casino clientele* / *Hotel night* / *Room price / night* — the
> operating cost lines.
- *Airport visitor fee* — actually a credit; see the airports page.
- *Cargo fleet purchase or sale* — the asset line.
- *Development bond issued* — when a bond is taken.
- *Bond repayments* — the monthly bond cost.
- *Financial aid sent* — when you support a neighbour.
- *Overdue fuel invoice* — the late-payment penalty.

> *Every dollar counts* — the flavor line.

## The budget report

The budget report is a monthly snapshot of every line item. Open it
from the management menu: **CITY PULSE** → **Budget & economy** → **Budget
reports**.

> *The budget needs attention* — the warning you'll see when the
> treasury is in trouble.

### The three views
- *Latest 12* — the last year of monthly snapshots.
- *Latest 36* — the last three years.
- *All time* — every snapshot since the city began.

### The line items
The report groups lines by category:

- **Income & cost breakdown** — the high-level summary.
- **Outlook income, including activity** — the projected income.
- **Outlook expenses, including activity** — the projected expenses.
- **MONTHLY OPERATING OUTLOOK** — the section header.
- **Money in** / **Money out** — the totals.
- **Residential & business taxes** — the tax line.
- **Road & rail maintenance** — the infrastructure line.
- **Service funding** — the services line.

> *Income earned* / *Net movement* — the day's movement.
> *Closing balance* / *Opening balance* — the daily open and close.
> *Next cash transactions* / *Previous cash transactions* — the
> upcoming and past.
> *Statement period* / *Reporting period* / *Air ledger period* /
> *Resource reporting period* / *Commerce reporting period* — the
> period labels for each subsystem.

### The settlement
- *Monthly settlement* — once a month, the books close.
- *Last settled household budget* — the household analogue.
- *Last settlement* — the last closed month.
- *Last month* / *This month* / *Previous month* / *Current month ·* —
> the period labels.

## Income groups and tax rates

Lundby has a unique feature: **separate residential tax rates by
household income group**.

> *Use income-specific residential tax rates* — the toggle in the
> tax controls.
> *Set separate residential rates for each income group.*

The three income groups are:

- *Lower income*
- *Middle income*
- *Affluent*

> *Slate: lower income. Green: middle income. Gold: affluent
> households.* — the household pin colours.

### The weighted average
> *The population-weighted residential rate is* — the summary that
> the game shows when you set per-group rates.

> *From affluent households* / *From middle-income households* /
> *From lower income households* — the per-group collection lines.

## Bonds and credit

> *Issue $25,000 bond* — the standard bond size.
> *The city can hold at most three active bonds.* — the cap.
> *Credit is unavailable while treasury is below −$50,000.* — the
> credit line.

Bonds are how you borrow money in the short term. They're repaid
monthly. The city can hold **up to three active bonds** at a time.

> *Bond paid off* — when a bond matures.
> *Bond repayments* — the monthly line.

## Development bonds

A separate kind of bond: **development bonds** for funding large
projects.

> *Development bonds* / *Development bond issued*
> *A new city charter costs $20,000 from this city's treasury.* —
> the cost of founding a new municipality in the region.
> *New city charter* — the action.

## Grants

> *Milestone grant* — the windfall you receive when you hit a
> population milestone.
> *Development grant · <milestone>* — the labelled version.
> *Financial aid received* / *Financial aid sent* — the regional
> line.
> *Capacity for 900 additional monthly visitors, shared across your
> cities.* — the regional cap on shared tourism.

## Tips & common pitfalls

- **Watch the operating outlook.** A positive outlook means you're
  growing sustainably; a negative one means you're spending faster
  than you earn.
- **Use the income-group rates.** A flat residential tax is regressive
  and can hurt happiness.
- **Don't issue a bond to fix a budget gap.** Bonds add monthly cost;
  they don't fix the underlying imbalance.
- **The rainy day fund is not a game mechanic — but the *treasury* is.**
  Keep a cash buffer for emergencies.
