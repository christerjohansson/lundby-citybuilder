# The terrain types

> *Every city is shaped by its land.*

Lundby gives you four terrain types to start from. Each has its own
character, its own opportunities, and its own traps.

## 1. Coastal bay
*Broad plains, a sheltered bay, and an eastern river. Room for a balanced
city.*

- **Best for:** your first city, a balanced economy, ferries and trade.
- **Opportunities:** marinas, cargo ports, ferries, waterfront property,
  the easy money of regional trade.
- **Watch out for:** floodwater. A coastal bay has a permanent water body —
  floods can build on it.

## 2. River valley
*A winding river divides fertile banks. Bridges and transit connect both
sides.*

- **Best for:** a city where the river is the centrepiece, with farmland on
  either side.
- **Opportunities:** farms on the floodplain, bridges as landmarks, tram
  and streetcar lines that hop the river.
- **Watch out for:** steep banks and bridges that interrupt the road
  network.

## 3. Highlands
*A mountain valley above a clear lake. Plan around slopes and protect the
forest.*

- **Best for:** a city with personality, where every block is shaped by
  elevation.
- **Opportunities:** scenic viewpoints, terraced housing, clean energy
  (wind, hydro), parks that lean into the terrain.
- **Watch out for:** steep grades. A guideway gap, steep grade, or submerged
  deck can interrupt a transit line.

## 4. Islands
*A chain of islands separated by channels. Connect them with bridges and
ferries.*

- **Best for:** a city where water is the constant, and your road network
  is a series of stitches between islands.
- **Opportunities:** sailing marinas, ferries between islands, regional
  trade by sea.
- **Watch out for:** every "edge of the map" is a coastline. Plan your
  regional road connection carefully.

## Map size

Each terrain comes in three sizes — **small**, **medium**, and **large** —
plus an **open-ended** option that gives you as much space as the terrain
will hold. Use open-ended for sandbox or for any city you plan to grow
past the standard limits.

## Tips & common pitfalls

- The terrain is the slowest thing to change later. Once you've built a
  city on highlands, every road pays the cost of the slope.
- You can raise and lower land with the terrain tools, but it's expensive
  and slow. Plan first.
- Each terrain has a slightly different default seed, which means a
  different starting road connection to the region.
