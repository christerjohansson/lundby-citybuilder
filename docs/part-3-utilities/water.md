# Water

> *From the ground to the tap.*

Water is the second utility every city needs. Lundby has a full water
cycle: extraction, treatment, distribution, use, wastewater, and
reclamation.

## The water cycle

1. **Extraction** — water towers, pumping stations, and groundwater.
2. **Treatment** — water treatment plants, membrane filtration.
3. **Distribution** — water pipes and the water mains under roads.
4. **Use** — buildings draw water for residents, industry, and fire
   suppression.
5. **Wastewater** — sewage is collected.
6. **Reclamation** — treated wastewater is returned to the aquifer.

> *Initial reserve + recharge + reclamation − pumping − overflow =* —
> the equation that drives the daily report.

## Water sources

### Water tower
*Place a water tower next to the road. Pipes are only needed away from
streets.*

- The basic source. Extracts water from a nearby aquifer.
- Needs a road, power, and an undeveloped site near water.
- *Green is a full aquifer. Orange is depleted ground. Wells share
  nearby reserves.*

### Pumping station
*Water pumping station*

- Higher capacity than a water tower.
- Best for large residential or industrial districts.

### Water treatment
*WATER WORKS*

- Treats raw water from the aquifer.
- *Membrane filtration unit* — a high-quality treatment annex.
- *Reduce water consumption by 30%.* — a research-driven boost.

## Distribution

### Water pipes
*Extends the water network across undeveloped land.*

- Place a water pipe to carry water away from a road.
- Pipes connect to other pipes, to buildings, and to water sources.

### Roads
Roads carry water beneath them by default. You only need pipes when you
want water to leave the road.

## The water map

The water data layer shows the water network.

- **Green** — water is available.
- **Orange** — water is contaminated or no supply.
- *Green building lots receive clean water. Orange means contamination
  or no supply.*

> *Clean water needs attention.*
> *Some taps are running dry.*
> *Drinking water is contaminated*

## Wastewater

Every building produces wastewater. The wastewater is collected, treated
if possible, and discharged.

- *Wastewater & reclamation* — the management menu section.
- *Sewage treated* — the daily report line.
- *Sewage treated locally* — for buildings with their own treatment.
- *Untreated sewage is reaching the ground* — a warning.
- *Untreated discharge* — the consequence.

> *Give sewage a safe route* — the message you see when a building
> has no working wastewater connection.

## Groundwater and aquifers

Lundby models the groundwater as a finite aquifer.

- *Aquifer allocation exceeds its reserve.* — a warning.
- *Local aquifer remaining* — the report line.
- *Citywide aquifer remaining* — the city total.
- *Soaked into ground* — water that's recharged the aquifer.
- *Initial reserve + recharge + reclamation − pumping − overflow =* —
> the equation at the top of the report.

> *Wells share nearby reserves.* — placing two wells close together
> means they draw from the same aquifer.

## Reclamation

Treated wastewater can be returned to the aquifer.

- *Water reclaimed underground* — the report line.
- *Overflow to the watershed* — what happens when the aquifer is full.

## Rain and surface water

*Rain & surface recharge*

- Rain recharges the aquifer and the surface water.
- *Soaked into ground* — the report entry.
- *Rainy day fund* — the treasury line. (Not a water concept, but the
  same word.)

## Tips & common pitfalls

- **Place water sources early.** A new residential block without water
  is a block that doesn't grow.
- **Pair water with power.** Most water sources need power to pump.
- **Treatment is a separate step.** Pumping untreated water gives you
  contaminated drinking water.
- **Reclamation matters.** Without it, the aquifer runs dry.
- **The aquifer is finite.** A growing city will run out unless you
  invest in treatment and reclamation.
