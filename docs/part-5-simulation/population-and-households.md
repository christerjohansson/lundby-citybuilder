# Population and households

> *A city of every generation.*

Population is the most important number in your city. It drives demand,
revenue, services, and growth. Lundby tracks population at the household
level, not just the city level.

## Population at a glance

- **Population** — the current resident count.
- *YOUR CITY IS HOME TO* — the flavor line on the title bar.
- *RESIDENTS* — the report label.
- *Highest population:* — the all-time peak.
- *Households* — the count of distinct households.

> *A city that takes care.* / *A city of every generation.* / *A city
> finding its rhythm* — the milestone flavor lines.

## Households

Every home is a household. A household has:

- **Income** — *Lower income*, *Middle income*, or *Affluent*.
- **Working age** — yes or no.
- **Children** — yes or no.
- **Seniors** — yes or no.
- **Living allowance** — the household budget.

> *Affluent* / *Middle income* / *Lower income* — the income groups.
> *Working age* / *Children* / *Seniors* — the demographic markers.
> *HOUSEHOLD CONDITIONS* — the report section.

## Happiness

Happiness is the result of jobs, services, and clean utilities.

> *Maintain 90 % happiness with 1 000 residents.* — the milestone.
> *Doing well* / *Thriving* / *Critical* — the happiness levels.
> *A safer neighborhood.* — the safety flavor line.
> *Happiness* — the report.

### What affects happiness
- **Employment** — a job in walking distance is a happy resident.
- **Services** — covered by fire, police, and medical.
- **Utilities** — connected to power, water, and waste.
- **Pollution** — high pollution is unhappy.
- **Taxes** — high taxes are unhappy; low taxes are happier.

## Tips & common pitfalls

- **Population follows happiness.** A clean, well-served city grows;
  a polluted, under-served city shrinks.
- **Households need shops.** A residential block without a walkable
  commercial area is one that loses residents.
- **Seniors and children are not workers.** A city with too many
  dependents and too few workers will struggle.
- **Watch the income mix.** A city of all lower-income households
  can't afford the services that keep them happy.
